Front matter

How to Use This Book

This book treats artificial intelligence as an encounter, not a product manual. You will not find step-by-step instructions for a specific app here, because apps change every few months. Instead, you will find stories, ideas, and tools that help you decide how and whether to use AI in ways that keep your thinking sharp and your relationships real.

an encounter, not a manual.

Every chapter follows the same four-part rhythm

1

The Encounter

A short story about a real-feeling person facing an AI moment. It puts you inside the problem before any theory arrives.

5 minutes
2

The Human-Centered Inquiry

The big ideas. Thinkers from philosophy, sociology, education, and computer science help explain what is really happening in the story.

20–30 minutes
3

The Core Competency

Connects the chapter to one of the career-readiness competencies employers ask for most, so the ideas travel beyond the classroom.

5 minutes
4

The Signature Tool

A worksheet, canvas, or protocol you can use right away, alone or with a group.

30–60 minutes

Each chapter closes with Key Terms, Questions for Discussion, and a Try This activity that takes the tool into real life.

Who are you, reading this?

If you are a student

Read the Encounter first and ask yourself whether it has happened to you. Then fill in the Signature Tool honestly, using your own coursework or job search. The tools are designed to be kept: many readers build a portfolio from them that they can show in interviews.

your route:Encounter→Signature Tool→My toolkit (your portfolio)

If you are an instructor

The book maps onto a 15-week semester: one chapter per week, plus an orientation week, a midterm workshop, and a final showcase. The five Acts also work as standalone units, so a writing course might use only Act III, while a career-development seminar might use Acts I and IV. The Postscript explains how to adapt each tool for general education, capstones, and career centers.

your route:Act III for writingActs I+IV for careers→Postscript

If you lead a department or institution

Start with Act V, then return to Act I. The leadership chapters make the most sense once you have seen how AI feels from the student's and worker's side of the desk.

your route:Act V→Act I

If you are simply curious

Read it straight through. The book is built as a journey: from the history of work, into the mind, out into relationships, onto the job market, and finally into the institutions that shape all of us.

your route:I→II→III→IV→V

A note on the stories

The Encounters are composite scenarios drawn from common experiences in classrooms and workplaces. Names and details are illustrative. They are meant to feel familiar, because some version of each one is happening somewhere right now.

Preface

Your First Encounter

If you are reading this book, you have already had an AI encounter. Probably several.

If you are an educator

Maybe your encounter was a moment of shock, the afternoon you first saw what generative AI could do. Maybe it came while reading a book like Ethan Mollick's Co-Intelligence, when you felt a sharp pinpoint of fear and excitement at the same time. Or maybe it arrived quietly: a student essay, a slide deck, or a website that felt a little off. Uncanny, in a way you could not quite name.

Whatever it looked like, it probably left you with questions about connection. How do you connect with students through assessment, course design, and feedback when both sides can generate those things in seconds? How do you link the work of your discipline to the skills students will need after graduation? How can educators think, learn, and teach with AI ethically and intentionally, so that it deepens human connection instead of bypassing it?

If you are a student

Your encounters may look different. Maybe one came late at night, up against a deadline, when you just needed the assignment done and were not sure why it had been assigned in the first place. Maybe it came the first time you spoke to your phone and it answered like a person. Maybe it was the familiar search engine reshaping itself into something new: an ask instead of a search.

Or maybe your encounter was a feeling in your gut. A stirring that told you there was something important to hold on to, something worth preserving about yourself and the world you are walking into.

You may be asking: What is my education worth when answers, knowledge, and even thinking itself are a few prompts away? How do I connect with mentors when they can generate feedback as easily as I can generate essays? What happens to friendship, community, and life both online and in real life as more of the world runs through artificial tools?

If you are a leader

Your questions are about scale. How do you build trust with stakeholders, create genuine buy-in, and shape a future where everyone feels heard, valued, and able to take part in decisions?

We also share the story of the Human-Centered AI program at UNC Charlotte, an interdisciplinary B.A. major and Artificial Intelligence minor rooted in the humanities and social sciences. It serves as a roadmap for educators, administrators, and leaders who want to build something similar. And we share stories from students who are learning about AI, working with it, and applying it to real problems, becoming decision makers and ethical stewards rather than passive users.

Like a thread weaving between us, this is a story about bringing people together to think, work, play, explore, learn, design, and build alongside a technology that will change our world.

To thrive on the journey ahead, we need to meet AI in new ways and use each encounter to remind us how to stay connected.
